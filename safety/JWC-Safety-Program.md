## Safety mission statement

Our mission is to deliver oilfield pipe, equipment and heavy loads safely, reliably and responsibly throughout Alberta. We will protect our people, our customers, the public and the environment through sound planning, competent workers, properly maintained equipment and clear communication.

Safety starts before a truck moves. We will identify hazards, respect driving and working limits, secure every load correctly and stop when conditions are unsafe. No schedule, delivery or production demand is more important than sending every person home safely.

As Chief Executive Officer, I am accountable for providing the leadership, resources and support needed to put this program into practice. Every worker is expected and empowered to take part, report concerns and stop unsafe work without fear of retaliation. We will learn from our work, correct problems and continually improve.

## 1. Management policy

JWC Contracting Ltd. will plan and perform its work to protect workers, customers, the public, property and the environment. No delivery, production target or customer instruction takes priority over safe and lawful work. JWC will provide competent supervision, suitable equipment, required training and resources to identify hazards, control risk and maintain safe vehicles.

Every person has authority to stop unsafe work. Workers may raise concerns, participate in safety decisions and refuse dangerous work without retaliation. Reporting a hazard, fatigue, impairment concern, incident or near miss in good faith will not itself result in discipline. Management will investigate concerns, correct hazards and communicate the outcome before affected work resumes.

This program establishes JWC's company rules and procedures. Where legislation, an operating permit, manufacturer instructions or an applicable site requirement is more protective, that requirement applies. A conflict must be resolved by management before work proceeds; a client cannot waive the law. This document does not represent a regulator-issued approval, COR/SECOR certificate, or a substitute for implementing the required controls and records.



## 2. Scope, legal basis and operating limits

This program covers JWC owners who drive, employees, dispatchers, supervisors, maintenance personnel, lease operators operating under JWC's authority, and contractors doing JWC-controlled work. It applies in the yard, on public highways, resource roads, lease roads and customer sites, including loading, unloading and related maintenance.

Authorized cargo includes non-dangerous pipe, casing, tubing, structural steel, skidded oilfield equipment, excavators and other machinery, attachments and related oilfield items. Load-specific requirements apply regardless of whether a move is described as an oilfield service or freight.

JWC's operating basis is an Alberta provincial carrier. Regulated vehicles require the applicable Safety Fitness Certificate, registration, insurance, inspections and written safety/maintenance programs. Provincial hours-of-service rules apply to Alberta-only commercial vehicles registered for 11,794 kg or more. Alberta's annual commercial inspection requirements apply to the regulated intra-provincial trucks, truck-tractors, trailers and combinations exceeding the prescribed registered GVW threshold of 11,794 kg. Management must verify each unit and combination against the actual regulation, not assume all requirements have the same threshold. JWC applies its daily inspection and hazard controls to its heavy-haul fleet regardless of a claimed exemption.

The legal register includes Alberta's Occupational Health and Safety Act, Regulation and Code; Traffic Safety Act; Commercial Vehicle Certificate and Insurance Regulation; Commercial Vehicle Safety Regulation; Drivers' Hours of Service Regulation; Commercial Vehicle Dimension and Weight Regulation; Vehicle Inspection Regulation; Vehicle Equipment Regulation; applicable road-use rules; and incorporated NSC Standards 10 and 13. The source register at the end provides current reference locations.

**Outside this program's authorized scope:** interprovincial/international operations, dangerous-goods transport, confined-space entry, live pressure-system work, and crane/picker operation without a separately approved plan and qualified personnel. The CEO must review the operating authority, law, insurance, training and procedures before any expansion. Oilfield work does not automatically qualify a vehicle or driver for an hours-of-service exemption.

The shipper must identify cargo and confirm its condition before acceptance. Equipment containing fuel, chemicals, pressure, contaminated residues or other regulated material must be assessed for dangerous-goods classification by a competent party. Do not assume used pipe, a tank, a skid or an apparently empty container is non-dangerous. JWC will not accept a regulated dangerous-goods load under this program. Normal vehicle operating fuel and workplace products still require their applicable handling controls.

## 3. Responsibilities and worker participation

**CEO — Jordan Cowan:** accountable executive and designated carrier safety and maintenance coordinator unless another competent person is appointed in writing. The CEO maintains the legal and permit register, approves drivers and equipment, funds corrective work, checks compliance, ensures required reporting, and reviews this program at least annually and after a serious incident, material legal change or change in operations. Delegation does not remove the carrier's responsibilities.

**Dispatch or the person assigning the move:** verify the driver is authorized, fit and legally available; confirm load weight/dimensions, route, access, permits, escorts, vehicle suitability, inspections and maintenance status; allow time for rest, inspections and securement; and arrange check-ins and emergency support. Never direct a driver to violate a limit, falsify a record or continue with a major defect. If the owner is driving, another available person must be assigned any required monitoring role.

**Driver/operator:** perform inspections, complete accurate duty records, assess hazards, measure/check the load and securement, follow permits and site controls, wear required PPE, and stop/report anything unsafe. A loader's approval or customer's signature does not release the driver from load-security responsibilities. Drivers must immediately report licence restrictions, suspensions, significant traffic charges, collisions, defects and conditions affecting safe driving.

**Maintenance personnel:** work within their competence, follow manufacturer and legal requirements, isolate equipment, document defects and repairs, and authorize return to service only after the required work and verification. Only authorized, suitably qualified persons may perform restricted trade work or CVIP inspections.

**All workers:** cooperate, attend orientation and toolbox meetings, report hazards and incidents, use controls and equipment properly, and protect others. Workers participate in formal hazard assessments and field reviews. At 5–19 regularly employed workers, JWC will arrange the required health and safety representative; at 20 or more, the required committee and OHS program arrangements. Multi-employer arrangements and prime-contractor obligations must also be checked. Small size does not remove the underlying OHS duties.

**Dangerous-work refusal procedure:** A worker who reasonably believes there is an undue hazard stops the affected work, protects others where reasonably possible and promptly tells the supervisor/CEO what is being refused and why. Management inspects and remedies the hazard immediately where possible. If it cannot be remedied immediately, stop the work, discuss the concern with the worker and conduct the required hazard inspection. Inform the representative/committee as soon as possible. Do not resume the work or assign another worker until the inspection is complete and the hazard is remedied or it is determined there is no hazard. Document the circumstances, inspection and action and give the report to the refusing worker and representative/committee as required. Safe alternative work must be within the worker's capability and without loss of pay. If the concern remains unresolved, the worker may contact OHS at 1-866-415-8690. No reprisal is permitted.

## 4. Hazard assessment, FLHAs and toolbox meetings

Before a new type of work is approved, the CEO and affected workers complete a documented task hazard assessment covering driving, securing/unsecuring pipe, machinery loading, winching, coupling, roadside work and maintenance. Identify exposure, who may be harmed, likelihood and consequence, existing controls, further actions, responsible persons and completion dates. Review after changes, incidents, new equipment or evidence that a control is ineffective.

Before each job or shift, and when conditions change, the crew completes an FLHA or equivalent documented field assessment. Review the actual work area, traffic, ground/support conditions, weather, visibility, overhead/underground services, load stability, pinch points, pressure/energy, site gas hazards, PPE, communication and emergency arrangements. Reassess after a new load, changed location, loss of visibility, equipment change, changed crew or unexpected hazard.

Controls follow this order: eliminate the hazard; substitute a safer method; use engineering controls; use administrative controls; then PPE. An unresolved high-risk condition means stop, isolate and contact management. PPE alone is not permission to accept an uncontrolled line-of-fire or crushing hazard.

Hold a toolbox meeting before non-routine or high-risk loading/unloading, at the start of a new site assignment and whenever a material change requires crew coordination. Record date, site, work, hazards, agreed controls/actions, leader and participants. Workers sign only after participating and understanding the controls. JWC's app forms may record FLHAs and toolbox meetings; dispatch must verify receipt and retain records. A form does not itself make an unsafe task safe. Use a paper backup if the app or connection is unavailable.

## 5. Driver qualifications, training and competence

Before assignment, management verifies the correct valid licence class and air-brake endorsement, medical/licence conditions, applicable experience and ability to perform the specific work. Obtain and review an appropriate commercial driver abstract at hire, dated within 30 days of hire, and obtain updated abstracts at least annually. Drivers must disclose changes between reviews; possession of a licence alone does not prove heavy-haul competence.

Each driver file includes an application or resume; the applicable preceding three-year employment history; licence and medical-condition verification; hiring and annual abstracts; safety convictions and administrative penalties; reportable collision history; orientation; training; practical evaluations; restrictions; corrective actions; and authorization for specific equipment. Record the actual licence expiry and recurring review dates privately in the driver register.

Orientation covers this program, worker rights, emergency arrangements, reporting, fatigue/HOS, inspections, cargo securement, permits, site hazards, PPE, communications, WHMIS and fit-for-duty expectations. Supervise inexperienced workers. Complete a documented practical evaluation before independent use of each unfamiliar truck/trailer configuration or loading method, and reassess at least annually and following an incident, unsafe performance or material change.

Training is task and site specific: Class 1/air-brake qualification as applicable; NSC 10 securement; daily inspection; provincial HOS; lowbed/jeep/booster configuration; winch/rigging competence where used; first aid as determined by the worksite assessment; H2S awareness/H2S Alive or client-accepted training where exposure or site rules require it; and client orientation/CSO where contractually required. Fall-protection or respiratory equipment requires the applicable training, fit testing and rescue arrangements before use. A client ticket such as CSO is not represented as a universal Alberta statutory licence.

Keep training and evaluation records, monitor expiry dates, and do not assign work beyond a person's demonstrated competence. Subcontractors must provide equivalent evidence for their assigned work.

## 6. Fitness for duty, impairment and conduct

Workers must arrive rested, alert and able to work safely. Alcohol, cannabis, illegal drugs, misused medication and any other impairment are prohibited while driving or doing safety-sensitive work. Workers must tell management about restrictions affecting fitness without being required to disclose unnecessary medical details. Management will arrange safe removal from the task, transport, assessment and accommodation as appropriate. Do not permit an impaired person to drive home.

Dispatch considers sleep opportunity, commuting, second jobs, night work, illness, medications, stress and consecutive shifts. Drivers must report fatigue and stop at a safe location when alertness declines, even if legal hours remain. Coffee, open windows or a customer's deadline are not fatigue controls. No worker may falsify a log, signature, inspection, training record or defect clearance.

Management will distinguish a good-faith report or error from reckless or deliberate misconduct. Corrective action may include coaching, retraining, restrictions, written warnings, removal from driving or further employment action appropriate to the facts. Serious unsafe acts can require immediate removal pending investigation. Discipline must not punish lawful refusal, injury reporting or protected participation.

## 7. Alberta provincial hours of service

**Dispatch and drivers must use Alberta provincial rules for the authorized Alberta-only operation, not a federal 14-hour/16-hour template.** The principal provincial requirements are:

- At least **8 consecutive hours off duty before commencing a work shift**, except where a specific lawful provision actually applies.
- No more than **13 hours driving in a work shift**.
- No driving after reaching the applicable **15-hour on-duty limit**. As an additional company rule, JWC ends driving no later than 15 elapsed hours after the first on-duty activity, even where breaks might otherwise affect the legal calculation. This company cap never extends a legal limit.
- After up to **4 consecutive hours driving**, take at least **10 consecutive minutes off duty or non-driving time**. The regulation alternatively permits more than 4 but no more than **6 consecutive hours driving** followed by at least **30 consecutive minutes off duty or non-driving time**. JWC normally schedules the 4-hour/10-minute option and earlier rest whenever fatigue requires it.
- No driving while fatigue, illness or another condition makes operation unsafe, regardless of remaining hours.

Loading, unloading, load checks, inspections, repairs, required waiting/readiness and other work that meets the regulation's on-duty definition must be recorded appropriately. Do not record waiting at a wellsite or loading yard as off duty merely because the truck is not moving. Work for other employers/carriers must be disclosed and accounted for. A meal, securement check or toolbox meeting is not automatically off duty.

Dispatch checks the driver's actual hours before assigning a move and during delays. Include repositioning, loading, securement, escorts, travel, unloading, inspections and the return leg. Drivers advise dispatch before a limit will be reached; dispatch arranges a safe stopping location, relief or rescheduling. No routine plan may rely on adverse-condition/emergency exceptions, split-rest provisions or an oilwell-service exemption. Any proposed exception requires prior legal verification, documented eligibility and the applicable permit; schedule pressure is not an emergency.

Provincial carriers are not currently subject to the federal ELD mandate merely because they operate heavy trucks within Alberta. JWC may use paper or a suitable electronic system that meets provincial record requirements and remains producible. The field ticket, pre-trip form or phone GPS is **not** an HOS logbook or compliant ELD by itself.

## 8. Daily logs, local time records and HOS monitoring

Unless all conditions for a lawful exemption are met, drivers maintain the required daily log with the prescribed graph/grid and information: date, driver and carrier/home-terminal details, vehicle identification, start time/location, duty-status changes and locations, distance/odometer details, totals and required fuel/supporting information. Sign the completed record. Maintain one true record per day; no duplicate competing logs or concealed work. Include days and information required by the applicable regulation, not only billable driving days.

For Alberta's **160 km local-record provision**, dispatch verifies all conditions: the driver stays within a 160 km radius of the home terminal, returns there and is released within 15 hours of shift commencement, and the carrier retains accurate report-to-work/release time records for six months. JWC's time record also identifies the driver/date and records driving and other duty sufficiently to verify limits and breaks. This is a log-format provision, not an exemption from driving limits, breaks or fatigue duties.

If any local-record condition ceases to be met, begin the required daily log immediately and include the preceding seven days' total on-duty information as required. Dispatch must not retrospectively label an over-radius move local.

Where daily logs are required, carry the current work shift and previous **two days'** original/duplicate logs and required supporting documents as prescribed by the provincial rule. Provide them to an officer on request. Forward original completed logs to the home terminal or principal place of business within **20 days**. Drivers retain duplicates neatly at their residence for at least **six months**; JWC retains logs/time records and supporting records at least six months and produces them as required.

For this small operation, the designated safety coordinator reviews **all submitted HOS records monthly**, with prompt review of known or suspected violations. Compare logs/time sheets with dispatch, fuel, trip inspections, permits, loading/unloading times and job records. Record missing entries, excessive hours, break failures and inconsistencies; obtain the driver's accurate correction while preserving the original/audit trail; document coaching or other action; and confirm the issue is resolved. An employer must not fabricate a driver's records.

## 9. Trip planning, dispatch and safe driving

No move is released until dispatch confirms: authorized driver and available hours; correct tractor/trailer configuration; legal weights and dimensions; cargo description and non-dangerous status; approved loading/unloading method; route and site access; required permits/escorts; inspection and maintenance status; weather/road restrictions; communication and emergency arrangements.

Drivers obey traffic laws, signs, permit conditions and lower site limits. Seat belts are required for every occupant in a moving vehicle. No unauthorized passengers. JWC prohibits handheld device use and other distracting activities while driving, including completing app forms at traffic lights. Stop and park safely before interacting with forms, maps or messages. Use required road-radio procedures without compromising control or attention.

Drive defensively, scan ahead, protect space around long and wide combinations, anticipate off-tracking, maintain a safe following distance and increase it for load, weather and road conditions. Do not use cruise control on slippery roads. Plan descents in a suitable gear, confirm braking capacity and use required brake-check areas. Never coast downhill or rely on an engine brake to compensate for defective service brakes. No aggressive driving or pressure to make up lost time.

Inspect the route for bridge capacity, clearances, turns, shoulders, grades, railway crossings, soft ground and access restrictions. Slow for washboard, frost, ice, mud, wildlife, dust and blind corners. Stop when visibility or traction is inadequate. Chains, traction aids and recovery equipment must suit the unit and be used in a protected location by trained workers. Do not enter an ice road, ice crossing or load-restricted structure without the operator's current authorization and capacity controls.

For railway crossings, verify loaded ground clearance, approach/departure geometry and the ability to clear the entire combination continuously. Arrange rail-authority coordination where required for a slow, oversize or grounding-risk move. Do not enter a crossing without sufficient clear space beyond it. If equipment becomes immobilized on the tracks, get people clear, immediately notify emergency services and the railway using the posted crossing emergency information, and do not attempt an exposed recovery in the path of a train.

## 10. Weights, dimensions, permits and escorts

Before each move, obtain reliable cargo weight, centre of gravity and lifting/loading information. Measure the **actual loaded combination**, including attachments and projections. Confirm axle groups, spacing, tire capacities, equipment ratings, registered weight, hitch/coupling ratings and trailer/jeep/booster configuration. The allowable load is limited by the lowest applicable legal, permit, road, registration or equipment restriction. Use scales when required to verify axle/gross distribution.

Dispatch obtains and reviews the applicable provincial oversize/overweight permit and any required municipal, bridge, resource-road, utility or site approvals **before travel**. Check seasonal road bans and road-owner conditions on the actual route and date. A provincial permit does not replace every other road-owner approval or make an unsuitable bridge, shoulder or access safe. Do not assume a divisible pipe load qualifies for a non-divisible-load exemption.

The driver carries the required permits and complies with the stated route, dates, dimensions, weights, speed, time/daylight, weather, signing, lighting, flags, escorts and restrictions. Brief the driver and escort personnel on route, radio channel, spacing, hazards, stopping places, public traffic and emergency signals. Check qualifications/equipment required by the permit. Stop for a route change, changed load, missing escort, failed communication or changed restriction until dispatch resolves it.

Overhead-line clearance must be verified using Alberta OHS requirements, Schedule 4 distances, line voltage and utility-owner arrangements. Never use a generic clearance distance for all voltages. No raising, loading, winching or travel into a prohibited approach distance without the required utility authorization and protective arrangements. Unknown clearance or voltage means stop and get it confirmed.

## 11. General cargo securement and inspections

All cargo must be contained, immobilized or secured under the applicable **NSC Standard 10** and Alberta requirements. It must not shift dangerously, roll, spill, leak, fall, blow off or compromise stability. The carrier and driver remain responsible when someone else loads the vehicle.

Use a load-specific securement plan based on actual weight, shape, centre of gravity, friction, supporting deck, restraint directions and attachment ratings. Calculate required tiedown number and aggregate working load limit using the correct Standard 10 rules and the correct credit for direct and indirect tiedowns. A chain's breaking strength is not its WLL. No one universal chain count or strap arrangement covers every oilfield load.

Inspect decks, bunks, stakes, dunnage, blocking, anchor points, chains, binders, straps and edge protection before use. The weakest rated component governs. Reject damaged, cracked, stretched, excessively worn, missing-tag/unverifiable or otherwise unserviceable equipment; do not repair a chain with bolts or wire. Protect straps from sharp edges. Restraints cannot rely on rotten dunnage, bent stakes, unrated attachment points or packaging bands alone. Secure loose accessories, covers and tools independently.

The driver checks cargo and securement **before departure**, **within the first 80 km after loading**, and then whenever duty status changes or after **3 hours driving or 240 km**, whichever occurs first. Use safe stopping places; check more often following rough roads, emergency braking, weather changes or evidence of movement. JWC does not rely on sealed-load exceptions for its open-deck oilfield work. Record checks with date/time/location and any adjustment. If movement occurs, stop, isolate the area and obtain a safe resecurement plan before continuing.

## 12. Pipe, casing, tubing and long steel procedure

Before loading, confirm dimensions, lengths, bundle integrity, mass, surface condition and contamination status. Agree on the loading arrangement with the loader and driver. Use a stable deck, suitably rated pipe bunks/stakes and positive blocking/chocking to prevent rolling and lateral movement. Arrange dunnage and tier support so the load is stable, supported and capable of being restrained. Protect the cab and consider forward movement; a headboard alone is not a complete securement system.

Keep people out of the roll path, between pipe and stakes, between bundles, under a lift and in the forklift/crane operating zone. Bundle bands and thread protectors are not lifting points unless expressly designed/rated for that use. Use suitable rated lifting attachments and competent loading personnel. Never stand on unsecured pipe or use hands/feet as chocks. Use mechanical assistance for handling heavy dunnage and restraints.

Apply the specific securement rules applicable to the cargo, plus sufficient rated tiedowns and end/lateral restraint for the actual arrangement. Do not assume rules for another commodity, such as concrete pipe, automatically cover steel oilfield tubulars. Account for stacked layers, polished/oily surfaces, loose pieces, unequal lengths and overhang. Confirm legal/permit projections and required markings before movement.

At unloading, park on stable, suitably level ground and establish an exclusion zone. Before loosening any tiedown or band, confirm the receiving bunk, loader and blocking will control every affected piece or bundle. Inspect for shifted cargo from a safe position. Release restraint in the agreed sequence from a protected location; never open a stake, cut a band or remove the final restraint while people are exposed to rolling pipe. If safe release cannot be demonstrated, stop and arrange mechanical stabilization.

## 13. Heavy equipment and skid loading procedure

Confirm machine weight, transport dimensions, centre of gravity, designated tie/lift points and trailer/ramp capacity. The trailer must have adequate deck support, ground clearance, axle capacity and stability for the loading path. Position on firm ground; apply brakes, chock where needed, control traffic and align approved ramps. Inspect detachable-neck connections, ramps, locks and support arrangements before loading. Follow the manufacturer's coupling/loading sequence; never improvise blocking or hydraulic support.

Only an authorized competent person operates the machine. One designated spotter controls the movement using agreed signals/radio; stop if contact is lost. No worker may stand between the machine and a fixed object, in front of or behind the loading path, beside an unsupported ramp, or under suspended equipment. Stop for ramp movement, slippery tracks/tires, unstable ground, inadequate lighting or uncertain capacity. Nobody rides on the transported machine.

After positioning, lower attachments to the deck or secure them as required, isolate movement, set the parking controls, secure articulation and attachments, and follow manufacturer transport instructions. Heavy vehicles/equipment/machinery weighing **more than 4,500 kg** require the applicable Standard 10 machinery-specific securement, including at least four tiedowns as prescribed; additional tiedowns may be necessary for weight and geometry. Use designated points and required restraint directions. Secure buckets, blades, booms and detached accessories separately as required; hydraulics or a parking brake alone are not cargo securement.

For skids, verify structural lifting/tie points, support the skid as designed, secure projecting components and assess any tanks, process residues or trapped pressure before acceptance. Do not use small pipework, handrails or unspecified lifting eyes as restraints. Recheck loaded dimensions, axle weights, permits, clearances and all locks before departure. Unloading reverses a planned safe process; stabilize the machine before releasing restraints and keep people clear.

## 14. Winching, rigging and recovery limits

Winching requires a documented job plan covering load/pull, slope, ground support, line geometry, winch and rope ratings, anchors, sheaves, trailer stability, controls and exclusion zone. Use manufacturer-approved equipment within its rated capacity and a competent operator. Inspect ropes, terminations, hooks, slings, shackles and anchors. Keep required wraps on the drum and use a controlled line path. No improvised towing from an unrated point.

Keep everyone clear of a tensioned line, rope bight, recoil area, sheaves, pinch points and the path of the load. Do not straddle or step over a tensioned line, guide rope by hand near the drum, shock-load the system, or stand between moving equipment. One signaller is designated; stop on loss of signal, snag, unexpected movement, poor spooling or uncertain load. Block/secure the load before releasing winch tension; a winch line is not an automatic substitute for transport tiedowns.

Do not use a winch as a crane or lift people. Crane/picker lifts, critical lifts and complex recoveries require a separately approved lifting/recovery plan and qualified providers/operators. Rigging must be suitable for the lift, inspected, rated and protected against damage. No person goes under a suspended load or into a position where an uncontrolled movement could trap them.

## 15. Site hazards, PPE and exposure controls

At arrival, obtain the site orientation and confirm the prime contractor/site contact, traffic plan, radio procedures, muster points, emergency signals, first aid, gas hazards and work permits. Coordinate simultaneous work and stop conflicting operations. The site operator controls process isolation; JWC must verify it is in place before work that depends on it. Never disconnect pressure piping, hoses or process equipment on an assumption that it is depressurized.

PPE is selected through the assessment and site rules: suitable head protection, eye protection, high-visibility clothing, protective footwear and task-appropriate gloves, with hearing, FR clothing, respiratory or fall-protection equipment where required. Inspect and replace damaged PPE. FR clothing is required where the fire/flash hazard or applicable site rule requires it; it is not a substitute for controlling the ignition source.

Where H2S or other hazardous atmospheres may occur, obtain the site's gas-control plan and use the required training, approved monitor, bump testing/calibration and alarm response. No reliance on smell. On an alarm or suspected release: stop safely, warn others, leave by a safe upwind/crosswind route to the designated muster point, notify the site contact and await clearance. Do not attempt untrained rescue or enter a release area. Respiratory protection requires the applicable selection, fit test, training and rescue arrangements; this program does not authorize entry into an unknown or immediately dangerous atmosphere.

Use designated access/three-point contact. Keep decks clear and icy/muddy access controlled. Avoid climbing a load; use ground-operated tools or a suitable work platform. Provide fall protection and a workable rescue plan wherever required by the actual hazard and Code, including lower-height hazards where applicable. No confined-space entry under this program.

Use lifting aids and team handling for awkward chains, dunnage and tools; do not twist under load. Control exhaust, diesel particulate, dust, noise and product exposures through assessment, ventilation and other controls before relying on PPE. Obtain WHMIS/SDS information for workplace products. Protect workers from heat/cold with clothing, shelter, hydration and planned breaks; stop when exposure or visibility cannot be controlled.

## 16. Backing, parking, coupling and roadside work

Plan to avoid reversing. Before backing, get out and look from a safe location, check overhead/side/rear clearances and ground support, set an exclusion zone and use a spotter where pedestrians, blind areas, congestion or site rules require one. Agree signals; only one signaller directs the driver. Stop immediately if the spotter disappears or anyone enters the zone. Never assume a backup alarm or camera proves the path is clear.

For coupling/uncoupling, use firm supporting ground, apply brakes/chocks as required, support the trailer correctly and keep people clear of crush zones. Inspect connections, locking mechanism, kingpin/fifth wheel, air/electrical lines, landing gear and safety devices. Confirm engagement by the manufacturer's visual checks and controlled tug test. Complete brake/connection checks before movement. Keep hands away from mechanisms during adjustment and follow the approved sequence for multi-unit combinations.

Park without obstructing traffic/emergency access. Prevent unintended movement; do not rely on hydraulics alone when working under or supporting equipment. Roadside breakdowns require a safe stopping position, warning devices and traffic protection where safe and legally required. Call dispatch, emergency services or a qualified recovery provider as needed. Do not place a worker in traffic to protect equipment or attempt an exposed repair without adequate controls.

## 17. Daily trip inspection and defect control

Before dispatch and at least within each required 24-hour inspection period, an authorized competent person inspects the tractor/truck and each applicable trailer, jeep, booster and other combination component using the applicable **NSC Standard 13, Part 2, Schedule 1** and Alberta requirements. A previous valid report is not permission to ignore a newly observed defect. A changed unit/combination requires confirmation of valid inspections for every component and a connection/brake check.

Record carrier, vehicle identification, odometer/hubometer, inspection location, date/time, inspector and report-maker names/signatures, and each defect or a statement that no defects were found. Inspect all applicable systems, including air brakes/protection, steering, tires/wheels/fasteners, suspension, coupling, frame, lights, controls, emergency equipment, glass/wipers and cargo securement. Brake work and measurements are performed only by competent authorized persons.

Report major defects to the carrier **without delay**. Do not drive until the major defect has been corrected and the required certification recorded, or a person legally authorized under the regulation has certified that correction is unnecessary. A driver's opinion alone does not waive a major defect. Report other defects in a timely manner, no later than the next required daily inspection, and record/report defects discovered during travel. Any condition that makes operation dangerous means stop, regardless of its label on a form.

Dispatch records the defect, responsible repair provider, required action, completion and return-to-service decision. Tag/lock out an unsafe unit so it cannot be reassigned. Keep the current report and applicable schedule available in the vehicle in an accepted, legible form; if electronic access/format is not acceptable or reliable, carry a paper copy. The app creates an inspection record; it does not perform the physical inspection or certify roadworthiness.

Send originals to the home terminal within **20 days**. Deposit reports at the principal place of business within **30 days of receipt** and retain them chronologically by vehicle for at least **six months after receipt**. Annual CVIP does not replace daily inspections or preventive maintenance.

## 18. Preventive maintenance, CVIP and return to service

The CEO maintains a fleet register identifying every unit, VIN, make/year, plate, configuration, service provider, registration, CVIP expiry and time/km/hour-based service requirements. Before a unit enters service, create its written maintenance schedule using manufacturer requirements, the applicable Alberta Schedule 2 requirements, duty cycle, age and condition. No unit is dispatched without an established schedule and a known next-due date/reading.

JWC's baseline is a **documented comprehensive maintenance inspection at least monthly or every 10,000 km, whichever occurs first**, with manufacturer or competent maintenance-provider requirements applied sooner where needed. This is a company inspection interval, not a claim of a universal statutory interval or a replacement for manufacturer servicing. For a low-use unit, time controls still apply. Shorten intervals for severe oilfield, mud, dust, washboard, heavy-load or winter service. Include lubrication, oils/filters, brakes, air systems, wheels/tires/bearings, suspension, frame/deck, ramps/necks, coupling/landing gear, lights, safety systems and auxiliary/winch equipment as applicable. Manufacturer-specified lubrication/maintenance is performed before its own due time/km/hours, even if the monthly inspection is not yet due.

Track due items before assignment. Arrange the required **annual CVIP** inspections for regulated units before expiry at an appropriately licensed facility, and keep the required certificate/decal available. Confirm the inspection requirements for every trailer and configuration component. Complete additional inspections after damage, structural repair or other triggering circumstances where required. Neither a permit nor an unexpired decal authorizes an unsafe vehicle.

Maintenance records identify unit, date, odometer/hubometer or hours, work/inspection performed, defects, repairs, parts/service provider, verification and next due item. Act on recalls and manufacturer defect notices. Retain roadside inspection results and ensure required repairs/reports are completed within their stated limits. Drivers may not clear an out-of-service condition merely by submitting a new app report.

Use qualified providers, controlled work areas, chocks/supports and hazardous-energy isolation. Prove isolation before work; release stored air/hydraulic/gravity energy safely. Never work under a vehicle or raised equipment supported only by a jack or hydraulic system. No unauthorized structural welding, brake modification or rating changes. The designated maintenance coordinator verifies repair evidence and required inspection/certification before return to service.

## 19. Emergency preparedness, first aid and working alone

Before a job, identify its exact location/LSD/GPS and directions, site contact, 911/other emergency access, muster point, available first aid, transport method, nearest suitable medical care, communication coverage and rescue limitations. Assess required first-aid personnel, supplies and equipment under OHS Code Part 11 and Schedule 2 using worksite hazard, worker count and actual travel/access conditions. A generic truck kit alone is not proof that an isolated worksite's requirements are met. Confirm these arrangements before dispatch and communicate them to the crew.

Each active vehicle carries the legally and operationally required emergency warning devices, suitable secured fire extinguisher, first-aid supplies and seasonal survival equipment identified by the assessment. Inspect supplies and extinguisher condition and arrange required servicing. Workers fight only a small incipient fire if trained, equipped and assured of escape; otherwise evacuate and call for help. Do not fight a tire, pressurized-system or hazardous-material fire by improvisation.

For a collision, injury, fire, gas alarm, spill or breakdown: stop safely; protect life without entering an uncontrolled hazard; raise the alarm/call emergency services; give exact location, access and known hazards; notify dispatch/site control; muster/account for people; and prevent further exposure. Only trained, equipped responders perform rescue. Preserve evidence and the scene subject to the legal exceptions for rescue, prevention of further harm and other permitted actions.

Working alone requires a written assessment and a named, available check-in contact. As JWC's default, check in at departure/arrival and at least every **two hours of travel**, using a safe stopped position; when working alone outside the cab at a remote location, at least every **30 minutes**, or more often if the assessment requires it. No high-risk task may be performed alone where safe assistance/rescue is unavailable. Do not depend solely on a phone without confirming coverage; use radio/satellite or another reliable system where needed.

If a check-in is missed, the contact attempts communication immediately and no later than **10 minutes** after the missed time, checks last known location/site contacts, and escalates to management and the documented response arrangements. At **20 minutes**, initiate the job's overdue-person response unless contact is restored; call emergency services sooner whenever the risk or available facts indicate distress. These are company maximum response timings, not permission to delay an evident emergency. Practise emergency/overdue-person response at least annually and after a material change, and record lessons/corrections.

## 20. Incident reporting, investigation and return to work

Report all injuries, illnesses, collisions, load shifts, dropped/rolling loads, equipment failures, spills and near misses to dispatch/management immediately when safe. Obtain care first. Notify the customer/prime contractor as required, but do not assume that a customer's report satisfies JWC's legal duties. Drivers must cooperate with police and meet current collision-reporting requirements.

The responsible prime contractor, or employer where there is no prime contractor, must notify Alberta OHS **as soon as possible** for reportable events, including a worker fatality; a worker admitted or expected to be admitted to hospital because of a work incident; the specified serious/potentially serious uncontrolled explosion, fire or flood; crane/derrick/hoist collapse or upset; and building/structural collapse or failure. Emergency-room or urgent-care treatment alone is not the same as hospital admission. Contact OHS at **1-866-415-8690**. Secure/preserve a legally protected scene and related equipment/documents except for permitted actions or officer authorization.

Management determines/reporting responsibility with the prime contractor without delaying notice. Investigate reportable events and other significant incidents, involve workers and the representative/committee where applicable, identify direct and underlying causes, assign corrective actions with deadlines, and verify effectiveness. Submit an OHS investigation report as required/requested and make reports available to the required parties. A potentially serious incident meeting Alberta's criteria must be investigated and reported through the OHS online process after the investigation; it is not merely filed internally. Retain required OHS investigation reports at least **two years**.

Submit reportable WCB-Alberta injuries within **72 hours of becoming aware**, using the current WCB criteria; internal or client reporting does not replace WCB reporting. Provide suitable modified work based on medical functional abilities where available, maintain privacy and prevent retaliation. No worker returns to safety-sensitive duties until fit and authorized.

## 21. Violence, harassment, public interaction and welfare

Violence, harassment, threats, bullying, discriminatory conduct and retaliation are prohibited, whether from a worker, supervisor, customer or member of the public. Assess risks associated with isolated work, customer disputes, public traffic and other foreseeable circumstances with affected workers. Use respectful communications, safe meeting arrangements, reliable contact methods and withdrawal from threatening situations.

A worker facing immediate danger moves to safety and calls 911/site security as appropriate. Report other concerns to the CEO promptly; where the CEO is involved, the worker may contact OHS/police or another appropriate authority directly, and JWC must arrange a competent independent investigation rather than have the subject investigate the complaint. Investigate fairly, protect information as far as reasonably practicable, disclose only for required reporting/investigation/corrective action, and provide support and corrective measures. Explain procedures during orientation and review prevention measures annually and after an incident or material change.

Provide access to drinking water, sanitary/washing facilities and reasonable rest/warming arrangements through JWC or the site operator as required. Workers must know how to obtain help for physical or psychological health concerns without compromising safe operation.

## 22. Contractors, environmental protection and change control

Before engaging a carrier, loader, crane provider, maintenance shop or other contractor, verify the applicable operating authority, insurance, WCB status, qualifications, equipment inspections, relevant procedures and site requirements. Define who controls the work, who provides the spotter, isolation, lifting plan, securement and emergency response. Obtain evidence rather than assuming a certificate or client approval covers the actual task. Monitor performance and stop unsafe contractor work.

During fuelling, park safely, control ignition sources, shut off the engine before ordinary vehicle refuelling, prohibit smoking/vaping/open flames, attend the nozzle, prevent overfilling and use the required PPE/spill controls. Inspect fuel/chemical containers and lines. Keep suitable spill supplies for normal vehicle-product incidents, protect drains/water where safe and call the site/appropriate authorities when required. Do not attempt cleanup outside training or enter a hazardous release. Segregate/dispose of waste appropriately. Report leaks before dispatch; no uncontrolled discharge from a load is acceptable.

Management must assess changes in cargo, equipment, routes, client requirements, worksite hazards, staffing and law before implementation. Interprovincial work or a dangerous-goods request requires a new applicability review and approvals before acceptance. This program does not establish that JWC holds an exemption, special permit, environmental authorization, COR/SECOR or client prequalification not separately documented.

## 23. Records, monitoring and program review

The CEO controls the master revision and ensures workers have access to the current program and relevant procedures. Public copies show the revision/date; downloaded/printed copies must be checked against the current website. Internal records stay at JWC's Alberta principal place of business identified in its current carrier records and must be accurate, legible, retrievable and available to authorized officers within the applicable time. Electronic storage must meet legal production/access requirements; preserve paper where required. Do not publish driver medical data, identification, worker signatures, detailed emergency contacts or investigation records on the public website.

JWC's retention baseline is:

- **HOS logs, local time records and supporting records:** at least six months; drivers also retain required duplicates for six months. Retain longer where an investigation, contract or legal hold requires it.
- **Trip inspections:** at least six months after receipt, chronologically by vehicle; apply the 20-day forwarding and 30-day filing requirements.
- **Vehicle inspection, maintenance, repair and recall records:** current calendar year and four preceding calendar years; after permanent retirement/disposal retain at least six months and longer under JWC's policy or an applicable hold. Keep unit identification and due schedules current.
- **Driver files:** retain at least five years from creation/receipt as JWC's baseline, meeting the applicable statutory record categories and any longer requirement. Keep current licensing/abstract evidence and the required conviction/penalty/collision/training records.
- **OHS incident/PSI investigation reports:** at least two years; JWC retains other FLHAs, toolbox records, training/evaluations, internal inspections and corrective-action evidence for at least three years as company policy unless a longer requirement applies.

Monthly, the coordinator checks all HOS records, missing inspection reports, open defects, maintenance/CVIP due dates, permits and training/licence expiries. Review the carrier profile at least monthly, investigate new entries and correct inaccurate information through the appropriate process. Inspect the yard, loading equipment and securement inventory monthly; act immediately on critical defects. Keep an action register with issue, interim protection, responsible person, due date and verified closure.

Annually, the CEO and affected workers review incidents, near misses, inspections, HOS/defect trends, driver performance, contractor performance and legal/client changes. Record the review, update procedures and communicate/retrain on changes. Required controls must be implemented before a job starts, not left open until the annual review. Measure leading indicators such as completed assessments/inspections, timely repairs, training and closed actions, not only injury counts.

## 24. Operating registers and field checklists

Maintain the following working records privately, using JWC app forms, suitable paper forms or another accepted system. The CEO owns each register until responsibility is assigned in writing. Entries must describe actual people, units and arrangements; this policy is not evidence that an uncompleted register exists.

**Company/authority register:** legal carrier name; SFC/operating status and expiry where applicable; Alberta principal place of business and home terminal; insurance/WCB; responsible safety/maintenance person; contacts; approved service providers; current legal/permit sources.

**Driver register:** driver and authorized units/tasks; licence class/endorsements/conditions/expiry; hiring/annual abstracts; training and practical evaluations; review dates; restrictions and corrective actions. Use an acknowledgment recording the worker's name, program revision, orientation date, questions addressed, supervisor and worker signatures.

**Fleet register:** unit/VIN/plate/make/year; ratings/configuration; CVIP certificate/decal and expiry; OEM service tasks; time/km/hour intervals and next due readings/dates; defects; repairs and return-to-service evidence. No blank schedule may be treated as permission to operate.

**Move-release checklist:** cargo/non-dangerous status; reliable weight and dimensions; driver and available hours; suitable configuration/axles; inspected and in-date equipment; securement/loading plan; actual route/bans/clearances; permits and escorts; site/prime contact; first aid, communications, check-in and emergency arrangements. Release only when each applicable item is confirmed.

**Field records:** FLHA and toolbox sign-off; signed trip report; cargo checks; log/time record; weigh tickets and load/job documents; permits; defects/repairs; incident/near-miss report. A separate HOS system is required even when app tickets and safety forms are used.

**Emergency card for every remote move:** exact site/LSD/GPS and access directions; site/prime contact; named check-in contact and alternate; emergency number; muster point; first aid/transport; communication method; agreed check-in and overdue-person response. Verify it before departure and update it when the route/site changes.

**Action and audit register:** identified problem; hazard/interim control; responsible person; due date; corrective work; verifier and closure date; related training and program revision. Store supporting evidence with the relevant driver/unit/job.
